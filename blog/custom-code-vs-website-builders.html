Custom Code vs
Website Builders

By JahawiDevs

Wix, Squarespace, and WordPress are real tools. They've helped millions of businesses get online. But for a serious business that wants to grow, there's a ceiling — and most businesses hit it earlier than they expect.

What Website Builders Are Good At

Let's be honest first: website builders aren't bad. For a one-person freelancer testing an idea, or a small shop that just needs a basic online presence and has no budget at all, a free Wix site is infinitely better than nothing.

They're also genuinely fast to set up. No hosting configuration, no FTP, no code. Drag, drop, publish. That accessibility has real value, and it shouldn't be dismissed.

The problem starts when businesses grow — or when they have specific requirements that the template was never designed for.

The Hidden Cost of "Free"

Free website builders are rarely free for long. Wix's free plan shows Wix branding on your site — a banner at the top that tells every visitor you couldn't afford a real website. Removing it requires a paid plan. Adding a custom domain requires a paid plan. Removing ads requires a paid plan.

By the time you've unlocked the features a real business needs, you're paying a monthly subscription in USD — which, converted to KES, adds up quickly over years. Meanwhile, a custom-built site is a one-time investment that you own outright, with no recurring platform fees.

Performance and SEO

Website builders generate bloated code. Every visual element, every animation, every feature you didn't ask for but came bundled with the template — it all adds to the page weight. Slow sites rank lower on Google and lose visitors.

A custom-coded site contains only what it needs. No unused plugins, no template bloat, no third-party scripts injected without your knowledge. The result is a faster site that scores better on Google's Core Web Vitals — which directly affects your search ranking.

If SEO matters to your business (and for most businesses in Kenya, it should), this is a significant difference.

Design Limitations

Every website builder operates within constraints. You can move elements around within a template, but you can't fundamentally break the mould. The result is that every Wix site looks like a Wix site. Experienced eyes can spot the template immediately — and again, that signals "this business took a shortcut."

Custom code has no constraints. Every pixel, every interaction, every animation is a deliberate decision. The design is the brand, not a brand squeezed into someone else's template.

When Custom Code Makes Sense

If any of the following are true, custom code is the right choice: you want your site to look distinctive and premium; you care about page speed and Google rankings; you're planning to grow and don't want to rebuild from scratch in two years; you need specific functionality that no template supports out of the box; or you simply want to own your digital presence completely.

Most serious businesses in Kenya that are thinking long-term tick most of those boxes.
